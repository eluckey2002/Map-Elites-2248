Ideation

Missing board experiences

The current game has plenty of parameter variation. The real opening is to author boards around a recognizable decision: what must the player notice, protect, time, or sacrifice?

What the data says

Grounding Context

Of the 58 shipped levels, 31 are 5×8 and 24 are 5×7. Only two are 6×5, and one is 4×8. The four stored generator batches range across 25 rectangular sizes and contain 504 screened shapes, but they sample width, height, move budget, demand, and random blocker coordinates independently. That produces many combinations without saying what distinct player experience each combination is meant to create.

The project already contains the beginnings of a better vocabulary. profile-shapes.js measures opening-chain share, late-score backload, and single-chain spike. Owner play supplies the strongest qualitative signal: the narrow central choke amplified planning pressure and remained fun, and the latest fixed-seed run repeatedly built 1,024 survivors before a 53,120-point harvest.

The gap is not “more random boards.” It is a set of named board families whose topology reliably elicits different decisions. That reading matches procedural-level-design research on expressive range and mechanic-centric generation: diversity should be judged by the experiences produced, and layouts should be shaped around intended player actions.

Bank and Break

Two stone-shaped basins let the player build one valuable survivor on each side. A central ice gate thaws after several moves, turning two local puzzles into one board and creating the moment to connect the stored value into a decisive chain.

Basis
direct + external: Owner play demonstrates build-then-harvest; ice already supports timed topology changes. Gameplay-constraint PCG research supports designing from a desired action sequence.
Rationale
This is the clearest embodiment of the strategy the user just articulated: smaller construction moves create valuable tiles for a later large chain.
Downsides
If the gate opens too early, it is decorative. Too late, and the player cannot exploit it. Seed and thaw timing must be qualified together.

Forked Harvest

Permanent stones divide the lower board into two viable investment zones, but the move budget is tight enough that only one can fully mature. The player chooses a side early, then lives with that commitment.

Basis
direct: Level 51 was worth shipping partly because the same seed admitted two distinct winning strategies; DECISION-0005 says blocker value depends on topology.
Rationale
Most current levels ask “which chain is biggest?” This asks “which engine am I building?” and makes replay meaningful because the other fork remains tempting.
Downsides
The two forks must be comparably viable. If one is obviously better, the board becomes a disguised single route.

No-Blocker Nemesis

An open board whose fixed seed offers no giant opening chain and rewards one or two quiet builder moves. Its identity comes from the exact board sequence, not a new obstacle.

Axis
Strategic arc
Basis
direct: Fixed-seed level challenges now produce an exact shared opening, and profile-shapes.js already treats opening share as meaningful.
Rationale
It tests whether the game can create deep strategy without visual clutter. It is also the cheapest family to prototype because it needs only seed selection.

Funnel Sprint

Three narrow vertical lanes feed a shared lower payoff zone. The move budget is short, so the player cannot exhaust every lane; they must build just enough in two lanes and time the convergence.

Builder Pocket

A small stone-bounded side bay gives the player a place to park a 1,024 or 2,048 survivor while continuing to harvest the main field. The pocket reconnects through a narrow mouth, so taking the stored value back out requires planning.

P = protected pocket. The obstacle is useful: it creates custody for built value rather than simply removing cells.

Axis
Value-building affordance
Basis
direct: Owner play repeatedly creates power-of-two survivors for later chains; the accepted owner decision says blockers can help or hurt depending on topology.
Rationale
It makes “save this tile for later” visible in the board itself, encouraging a skill the current random layouts only permit accidentally.
Downsides
The pocket could become a trap or a free safety zone. Its mouth and refill behavior must preserve choice rather than guarantee value.

Value-building affordance

Tiny Marathon

A very small board gets a generous move budget. The challenge is not reaching a target quickly; it is maintaining a matchable value ecology without filling the board with permanent off-lattice survivors.

· · · ·
· · · ·
· · · ·
· · · ·

The geometry is deliberately plain. Scarce cells plus many moves make survivor quality, not obstacle routing, the board's identity.

Axis
Value-building affordance
Basis
direct: The ledger proves off-lattice survivors never become matchable again and accumulate toward lockout; current shipped levels do not make that long-horizon pressure the central experience.
Rationale
It turns an otherwise hidden system into the puzzle. Expert play should look visibly different because careless high-scoring chains poison future space.
Downsides
High frustration risk. A generous target or too many moves could make failure feel inevitable, and the current bot may be a poor authoring proxy for patient cleanup.
