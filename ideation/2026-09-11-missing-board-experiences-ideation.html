Missing board experiences

The current game has plenty of parameter variation. The real opening is to author boards around a recognizable decision: what must the player notice, protect, time, or sacrifice?

What the data says

Grounding Context

Of the 58 shipped levels, 31 are 5×8 and 24 are 5×7. Only two are 6×5, and one is 4×8. The four stored generator batches range across 25 rectangular sizes and contain 504 screened shapes, but they sample width, height, move budget, demand, and random blocker coordinates independently. That produces many combinations without saying what distinct player experience each combination is meant to create.

The project already contains the beginnings of a better vocabulary. profile-shapes.js measures opening-chain share, late-score backload, and single-chain spike. Owner play supplies the strongest qualitative signal: the narrow central choke amplified planning pressure and remained fun, and the latest fixed-seed run repeatedly built 1,024 survivors before a 53,120-point harvest.

The gap is not “more random boards.” It is a set of named board families whose topology reliably elicits different decisions. That reading matches procedural-level-design research on expressive range and mechanic-centric generation: diversity should be judged by the experiences produced, and layouts should be shaped around intended player actions.

What later playtests changed

Evidence update

The original seven directions remain below as the historical first pass, but later prototype play changed their standing. Bank and Break is the one retained distinct-play family. Funnel Sprint remains a routing hypothesis. Builder Pocket, No-Blocker Nemesis, Sequential Defusal Relay, and Tiny Marathon in its original form were retired because they either lost their identity after refill, made space feel wasted, let ordinary maximum-chain play solve the visible objective, or failed to name a choice beyond the owner’s normal build-and-harvest strategy.

The owner’s current observation supplies a sharper frontier: the board is often one compatible tile short of 2048, so the best available 1024 harvest may clear roughly ten fewer tiles and collapse planning into a single inferior line. The relevant question is not simply whether a level contains more large values. It is whether a named 2048 or 4096 frontier offers multiple materially different, replayable plans with distinct inventory costs and survivor locations.

Wait — adding all starting-tile ideas to the MAP-Elites genome now is not the best first move. It would mix three hypotheses, enlarge the search space, and require a receipt-sensitive schema extension before the current seed-and-geometry system has failed its control. First measure landmark frontiers and mine fixed seeds; promote authored starts only when that control cannot reach the desired behavior space.

Top pick · landmark access

Landmark Frontier Audit

At every named 2048 or 4096 frontier, count the materially different first decisions that retain a replayable route to the landmark within a fixed horizon. For each route, record cells cleared, score, deliberately built inventory consumed, survivor location, and the best fallback harvest. This makes “one tile short of 2048” an authoring diagnosis rather than a vague feeling.

route A → 2048 @ bank
route B → 2048 @ bridge
fallback → 1024 + space

compare:
inventory · cells · location

The routes count only when their first commitments and downstream consequences differ. Reversals and spatially identical outcomes collapse together.

Axis
Landmark access
Basis
direct: Measurement & Analysis Standards already defines landmark-route multiplicity and rejects legal-move count as a substitute for meaningful plans. Build potential is the named missing measure needed to price what each route consumes.
Rationale
This is the smallest reliable step. It can test the owner’s exact observation on current boards and tell us whether authored starts are necessary before the genome expands.
Downsides
A bounded search can miss routes and must report UNKNOWN. The measure describes structure; human play still decides whether the alternatives feel meaningful.

Landmark access

Three-Landing 2048

Offer several routes to the same 2048, but make each route spend a different bank and leave the survivor in a different berth: an open scoring lane, a protected reserve, or a bridge between rooms. The choice is not merely whether 2048 is available; it is which inventory should fund it and where the resulting asset should live.

BANK ← 2048
  ↑      ↑
reserve  bridge → OPEN LANE

Same landmark, different consumed inventory and survivor custody. That difference is what prevents three arithmetic recipes from being cosmetic variants.

Axis
Landmark access
Basis
direct + external: Landmark-route multiplicity requires non-equivalent plans; merge survival makes endpoint placement consequential. Triple Town supplies relevant prior art for completion location changing later play.
Rationale
This answers the 2048 scarcity observation without making the landmark free. More access creates more planning only when each access route carries a different future cost.
Downsides
The current schema cannot place exact route ingredients. Start with seed-mined versions; an explicit starting-layout capability remains a gated follow-up.

Persistence under refill

Seed-Mined Authored Starts

Before adding explicit starting tiles, mine fixed seeds for boards that already contain multiple landmark routes, then pair those seeds with stone or ice geometry that preserves the consequences of the first commitment. Compare the same seed on an open board to prove that the geometry—not merely a lucky deal—keeps the decision alive.

Axis
Persistence under refill
Basis
direct: Fixed seeds and blockers exist today; No-Blocker Nemesis showed that an authored opening disappears after one or two refills unless a persistent mechanism carries it forward.
Rationale
This is the proper control for the proposed schema extension. If the current system can create the desired frontier, we avoid widening the genome; if it cannot, the failure justifies new capability.
Downsides
Seed mining may find brittle or rare openings, and success on one seed does not establish a reusable family.

86%

High-value anchor custody

4096 Clearinghouse

Place a 4096 at a stone-supported junction between two banks. Either bank can feed the anchor, but doing so consumes that bank’s accumulated work and fixes the enlarged survivor at a strategically consequential location. Leaving the anchor dormant while the banks mature independently must remain a viable third posture.

Axis
High-value anchor custody
Basis
direct: Bank and Break showed that separated retained values can create a legible build-and-release arc. The engine’s endpoint rule makes the final survivor’s location matter.
Rationale
A starting 4096 becomes banked work plus positional debt—not free score. It asks which room should surrender value, where the next asset should live, and when the transaction is worth making.
Downsides
Requires an authored-start extension, and one bank may dominate in practice. The anchor must pay rent for the premium cell it occupies.
Confidence
68%
Complexity
High

Timed-threat conflict

4096 Bomb With Two Exit Plans

Put a 4096 bomb where two separate banks can rescue it before expiry. The fast route sacrifices mature inventory but preserves maneuvering room; the slower route spends moves and preserves the stronger bank for a later harvest. Both routes must leave a reusable survivor and remain target-viable.

Axis
Timed-threat conflict
Basis
direct + external: Sequential Defusal Relay proved that bombs swept by ordinary maximum chains are strategically empty. Double Vault Rescue and Bejeweled-style positional bombs show the stronger pattern: preparation and survivor value must compete with the deadline.
Rationale
The timer now prices investment rather than issuing a chore. The interesting decision is which bank to spend and what asset the rescue should create.
Downsides
Non-dominance is unproven. A short fuse forces one route; a long fuse makes the bomb decorative. This should remain behind the authored-anchor capability gate.
Confidence
62%
Complexity
High

Off-lattice recovery

Odd-Factor Escrow

Seed two recoverable off-lattice families in separate bays. The player can reclaim one immediately for space, mature one into a later asset, or bypass both through a normal-lattice route; developing every family should be unattractive. The unusual values act as recoverable obligations, not permanent blockers.

Axis
Off-lattice recovery
Basis
direct + external: The engine accepts equal-and-double off-lattice chains such as 6 → 6 → 12, reclaiming cells while preserving the odd factor. Triple Town’s obstacle-to-resource chain is a structurally relevant precedent.
Rationale
This could make space pressure into a portfolio decision: which arithmetic ecology deserves construction capacity, and which debt is rational to carry?
Downsides
Lowest-confidence survivor. Readability, partner-construction cost, bot competence, and whether the choice feels strategic are all unresolved. It should not enter the main genome before a controlled prototype.
Confidence
58%
Complexity
High

Persistence under refill

Three-Room Escrow Market

Extend Bank and Break to three separated rooms containing different partial assets. Move budget and staggered connections make fully developing all three unattractive, so the player selects a two-room portfolio and later decides which inventory crosses the shared corridor.

Axis
Persistence under refill
Basis
direct: Bank and Break is the one retained distinct-play family; its captured run reused seven built tiles and made the later connection legible. This extension preserves that verified mechanism while adding a non-binary allocation choice.
Rationale
It is the strongest near-term board hypothesis because current geometry can express it without authored tile values. It also tests whether “more than two plans” can remain legible under refill.
Downsides
Three rooms may become visually or cognitively noisy, and favorable refills could still dominate the intended portfolio choice.
Confidence
78%
Complexity
Medium

Bank and Break

Two stone-shaped basins let the player build one valuable survivor on each side. A central ice gate thaws after several moves, turning two local puzzles into one board and creating the moment to connect the stored value into a decisive chain.

Axis
Strategic arc
Basis
direct + external: Owner play demonstrates build-then-harvest; ice already supports timed topology changes. Gameplay-constraint PCG research supports designing from a desired action sequence.
Rationale
This is the clearest embodiment of the strategy the user just articulated: smaller construction moves create valuable tiles for a later large chain.
Downsides
If the gate opens too early, it is decorative. Too late, and the player cannot exploit it. Seed and thaw timing must be qualified together.

Forked Harvest

Permanent stones divide the lower board into two viable investment zones, but the move budget is tight enough that only one can fully mature. The player chooses a side early, then lives with that commitment.

Axis
Space and topology
Basis
direct: Level 51 was worth shipping partly because the same seed admitted two distinct winning strategies; DECISION-0005 says blocker value depends on topology.
Rationale
Most current levels ask “which chain is biggest?” This asks “which engine am I building?” and makes replay meaningful because the other fork remains tempting.
Downsides
The two forks must be comparably viable. If one is obviously better, the board becomes a disguised single route.

Builder Pocket

A small stone-bounded side bay gives the player a place to park a 1,024 or 2,048 survivor while continuing to harvest the main field. The pocket reconnects through a narrow mouth, so taking the stored value back out requires planning.

Axis
Value-building affordance
Basis
direct: Owner play repeatedly creates power-of-two survivors for later chains; the accepted owner decision says blockers can help or hurt depending on topology.
Rationale
It makes “save this tile for later” visible in the board itself, encouraging a skill the current random layouts only permit accidentally.
Downsides
The pocket could become a trap or a free safety zone. Its mouth and refill behavior must preserve choice rather than guarantee value.

direct, corrected: Off-lattice values have no partners in the ordinary spawn family, but compatible off-lattice values can be deliberately constructed and merged to reclaim cells. Their recovery cost and strategic pressure remain unresolved.
