# FR-0005 — target-aware promotion rehearsal lost its decisive diagnostic

- **occurred:** 2026-08-30
- **run:** `target-aware-promotion-rehearsal-2026-08-30`
- **candidate commit:** `ab8cbb5a381f3628a9084b738bc0836d1636fdef`
- **closeout commit:** `542e4d609b1153be5b96a0d8612802e8068ff34a`
- **outcome:** `RETAIN_CHAMPION`

## What failed

The promotion comparator evaluated all 300 Level 53 candidate games and then
applied a fail-fast cell comparison. It threw `Level 53 changed same-speed
winning outcome` before writing `promotion-verification.json`. The run retained
the safe non-promotion verdict, but not the first differing seed, its paired
terminal values, or the final exact/earlier/new counts.

The candidate was therefore shown not to satisfy the frozen promotion rule. It
was not shown to be worse: the lost pair could have had a higher or lower score.
Level 53 was not rerun because the protocol authorized one reveal only.

## Where and why

- **location:** historical `solver/promotion-replay.js`, inside the map from
  completed Level 53 rows through `validateLevel53Comparison`
- **failure class:** setup mistake / instrument failure
- **root cause:** the comparator coupled evidence persistence to successful
  verdict completion. Raw paired results existed only in memory, so a rejected
  cell prevented the evidence artifact from being written.
- **preserved evidence:** the historical sealed-reveal record had SHA-256
  `acc15f15fd87132d3343299b39083cc4d1037d65db5010ea757840b38bc980ec`;
  the write-once champion baseline had SHA-256
  `d8305fc9f3908ce767d4094c75363d25c4f77b7f53a49fc4af43031a4878bdde`.

## Prevention

`tools/persist-before-verdict.js` makes the sequence explicit: validate the
complete raw artifact, write it once, and only then evaluate the verdict. The
negative test in `solver/tests/persistBeforeVerdict.test.js` plants the same
failure shape and proves that all paired rows remain readable after the verdict
throws.

This prevents the evidence-loss mechanism. It does not decide whether a
same-speed score difference is good or bad; that remains a preregistered policy
choice for any future promotion experiment.
