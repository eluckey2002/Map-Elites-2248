## Findings

### Critical — accepted evidence overstates the game rule

`FACT-0006` says an off-lattice tile is something “nothing can ever match again” and that such tiles “never leave.” The live rule permits any two equal values to start a chain (`solver/engine.js:68-80`). A controlled state containing two six-valued tiles at scale 1 produced a legal two-tile chain: both sixes matched, merged into one twelve, and reduced the off-lattice cell count from two to one. Off-lattice mass cannot return to the power-of-two spawn lattice through these positive sums, but equal off-lattice tiles can match and their occupied-cell count can shrink. `CURRENT.md:17` repeats the stronger false claim. Confidence: exact source rule plus executed counterexample.

### Critical — the formula does not measure the owner's construct

The owner clarified that experienced pressure came predominantly from planning and routing ordinary on-lattice chains: current length/value, when to cash them, survivor placement, future chain opportunity, and the combined value over at least two moves under topology constraints. `strandedCellPressure` counts only off-lattice occupancy. It discards the dominant stated mechanism. Confidence: owner semantic authority plus direct formula inspection.

### High — the measurement includes decisions after live play ends

The browser ends immediately when the target is reached (`src/game.js:667-673`), while `playToBudget` deliberately continues for uncensored score (`solver/policy-eval.js:45-76`). In the exact SPR-001 trace, `1,264/2,844` off-lattice cell-observations—44.4% of the numerator—occurred after the target had already been reached; another 136 occurred on the terminal move and could not constrain a subsequent live decision. The code behaves as written, but this seam is not a clean measure of player-experienced pressure. Confidence: exact source boundaries plus fixed-panel trace.

### High — the durable aggregate test can false-PASS with a shared bug

`solver/tests/policy-eval.test.js:112-120` derives expected summaries with the same production `summarizePostMoveTrace` helper used by the code under test. If that helper returned a constant zero, the played summary, recomputed summary, and evaluated summary could all agree and the range assertions would still pass. The exact literal tests protect individual observations, not multi-observation aggregation. The audit independently checked aggregation, but that evidence is not yet a durable regression test. Confidence: direct test dependency analysis.

### Medium — policy aggregation silently weights moves rather than games

`evaluatePolicy` adds every post-move observation to one total and divides by observation count. A longer-lasting game therefore contributes more weight than an early lockout or bomb failure. That is a coherent “average played move” definition, but it was not semantically reviewed as the desired policy-level weighting. Confidence: exact aggregation code.

### Medium — the lattice predicate has a dormant 32-bit coercion defect

`isMergeableSum` uses JavaScript bitwise arithmetic. It correctly classifies current values, but it classified `3 × 2^32` and `5 × 2^32` as powers of two; a two-cell `[2^32, 3 × 2^32]` subject returned pressure `0` instead of independently expected `1/2`. Current shipped levels are not exposed: the audited real traces peaked at `14,272`, and a conservative bound from the current maximum 40 cells, 32 moves, scale 32, and spawn maxima is `339,968`. Confidence: executed boundary counterexample and current-level bound.

### Medium — one investigation receipt mislabeled a different aggregate

OL-001 called `2,844 / 46,928 = 0.06060347766791681` a reproduction of overall panel pressure. Production averages per-move fractions and gives `0.06205316319536222` across the equally observed policy aggregates. OL-001 now carries an append-only correction; the raw origin and timing counts were not affected. Confidence: exact arithmetic and source aggregation.
